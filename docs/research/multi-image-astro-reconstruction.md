## 0. Framing the problem

The target problem is: given *N* photographs of the same patch of sky, taken by different people, from different places on Earth, at different times, with different sensors, lenses/telescopes, filters, exposures and tracking quality, produce a single image that is better than any input.

"Better" decomposes into four separable quantities, and it matters enormously that they are separated, because they have very different feasibility:

1. **Signal-to-noise / depth** — how faint an object is detectable. *Scales well with N. This is the easy, guaranteed win.*
2. **Sampling** — recovering spatial frequencies that individual frames alias away because their pixel grid is too coarse. *Genuinely recoverable from sub-pixel-diverse inputs. This is the real "super-resolution" win.*
3. **Angular resolution beyond the optical cutoff** — resolving detail finer than lambda/D of the best contributing aperture. *Not recoverable by incoherent combination. Only modest, prior-dependent gains via deconvolution.*
4. **Dynamic range, color fidelity, time-domain information** — *recoverable, and undervalued.*

Almost all confusion in this space (including the Event Horizon Telescope analogy) comes from conflating (2) with (3). Section 5 addresses this directly.

## 1. Multi-frame / burst super-resolution (learned)

This is the closest mature computer-vision literature. The canonical framing: a handheld burst of RAW frames with sub-pixel hand-tremor offsets is fused into one higher-resolution RGB image.

**Handheld Multi-Frame Super-Resolution** — Wronski, Garcia-Dorado, Ernst, Kelly, Krainin, Liang, Levoy, Milanfar. *ACM TOG 38(4):28, SIGGRAPH 2019.* [DOI 10.1145/3306346.3323024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3306346.3323024). The algorithm behind Google Pixel's Super-Res Zoom and Night Sight merge. It replaces demosaicing entirely: each RAW Bayer frame contributes irregularly-sampled color samples into a common grid, accumulated by locally-adaptive anisotropic Gaussian kernels whose shape is steered by the local image gradient structure tensor, with a robustness model that down-weights samples inconsistent with the base frame (this is what prevents ghosting from motion). Conceptually it is *kernel regression from scattered samples* — i.e. a smarter drizzle. Two solid unofficial implementations: [Jamy-L/Handheld-Multi-Frame-Super-Resolution](https://github.com/Jamy-L/Handheld-Multi-Frame-Super-Resolution) (Python/Numba GPU) and [kunzmi/ImageStackAlignator](https://github.com/kunzmi/ImageStackAlignator) (C#/CUDA). **This is the single most transferable non-astronomical method for your product.**

**Deep Burst Super-Resolution** — Bhat, Danelljan, Van Gool, Timofte. *CVPR 2021.* [arXiv:2101.10997](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10997). Introduced DBSR (explicit optical-flow alignment + attention-based weighted fusion in feature space) and, more importantly, the **BurstSR dataset**: 200 real RAW bursts of 14 frames from a Galaxy S8, each paired with a tripod-mounted DSLR high-resolution ground truth, plus **SyntheticBurst**, generated by degrading the ZurichRAW2RGB corpus. Code: [goutamgmb/deep-burst-sr](https://github.com/goutamgmb/deep-burst-sr).

**Deep Reparametrization of Multi-Frame Super-Resolution and Denoising** — Bhat, Danelljan, Yu, Van Gool, Timofte. *ICCV 2021 (oral).* [arXiv:2108.08286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.08286). Theoretically the most important of the line for your purposes: it takes the classical MAP multi-frame objective (data-fidelity per frame through a forward model, plus a prior) and reparametrizes it into a learned deep feature space, solving it with an unrolled optimizer. It keeps the principled per-frame weighting of classical multi-frame fusion — which is exactly what you need when frames are wildly unequal in quality — while learning the error metric and prior. Code: [goutamgmb/deep-rep](https://github.com/goutamgmb/deep-rep).

**BIPNet: Burst Image Restoration and Enhancement** — Dudhane, Zamir, Khan, Khan, Yang. *CVPR 2022 (oral, best-paper finalist).* [arXiv:2110.03680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03680), code [akshaydudhane16/BIPNet](https://github.com/akshaydudhane16/BIPNet). Pseudo-burst feature fusion + edge-boosting deformable alignment; SOTA simultaneously on burst SR, burst denoising, and low-light burst enhancement.

**Burstormer: Burst Image Restoration and Enhancement Transformer** — Dudhane et al. *CVPR 2023.* [arXiv:2304.01194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01194), code [akshaydudhane16/Burstormer](https://github.com/akshaydudhane16/Burstormer). Transformer with cyclic burst sampling for inter-frame communication and reference-based feature enrichment; multi-scale local + non-local features.

**BSRT** — Luo et al. *CVPRW/NTIRE 2022*, winner of the real-world track. [arXiv:2204.08332](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08332), code [Algolzw/BSRT](https://github.com/Algolzw/BSRT). Pyramid flow-guided deformable convolutions + Swin Transformer backbone.

**RBSR: Efficient and Flexible Recurrent Network for Burst Super-Resolution** — Wu et al. *PRCV 2023.* [arXiv:2306.17595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17595), code [ZcsrenlongZ/RBSR](https://github.com/ZcsrenlongZ/RBSR). **Directly relevant architectural property:** it fuses frames one at a time recurrently, using the base frame as a persistent prompt. This makes it *flexible in N* — it accepts an arbitrary and variable number of input frames, which a fixed-input CNN cannot. A photo repository has a wildly variable N per target (3 for an obscure field, 5,000 for M42).

**BurstM: Deep Burst Multi-scale SR using Fourier Space with Optical Flow** ([arXiv:2409.15384](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.15384)) and **Burst Super-Resolution with Diffusion Models** ([arXiv:2403.19428](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.19428)) extend to arbitrary (non-integer) scale factors and to perceptual-quality generative refinement respectively. The latter is a cautionary case for astronomy: perceptual gains come with hallucination.

**NTIRE challenge reports** — [NTIRE 2021 Burst SR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.03839) and NTIRE 2022 Burst SR provide the standard benchmark protocol and a snapshot of the competitive field; repos at [goutamgmb/NTIRE21_BURSTSR](https://github.com/goutamgmb/NTIRE21_BURSTSR).

**Critical caveat for transfer.** All of the above assume: same camera, same focal length, same exposure, sub-second temporal span, translations of <= a few pixels, no field rotation, no atmospheric variation, and an alignment problem solved by learned optical flow. Your setting violates every one of those. **But it violates them in a helpful direction**: you have *astrometry*. A plate-solved WCS gives geometric registration to a small fraction of a pixel analytically, which is more accurate than any learned flow. The right adaptation is to strip the alignment module out of these architectures and replace it with WCS-driven resampling, leaving the network to do only fusion, deconvolution and denoising.

## 2. Reference-based, unaligned, and heterogeneous multi-image SR

This smaller literature is the one that actually matches "different cameras, different scales, different orientations."

**A graph neural network for heterogeneous multi-image super-resolution (Magnet++)** — Tarasiewicz, Kawulok et al. *Pattern Recognition Letters 189 (2025) 214.* [DOI 10.1016/j.patrec.2025.01.028](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.patrec.2025.01.028). The most on-point paper in the whole survey. It represents a set of low-resolution observations as a **graph** rather than a stacked tensor, giving two properties CNN burst methods cannot have: permutation invariance over frames, and native handling of inputs at **different scales and orientations** without pre-rescaling them to a common size (which destroys information). For a repository where a 200 mm lens shot and a 2000 mm telescope shot of the same nebula must both contribute, this is the correct inductive bias.

**SuperF: Neural Implicit Fields for Multi-Image Super-Resolution** — Jyhne, Igel, Goodwin, Andersen, Belongie, Lang. *ICLR 2026.* [arXiv:2512.09115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09115), project page [sjyhne.github.io/superf](https://sjyhne.github.io/superf/). Test-time optimization: a single coordinate-based implicit neural representation is shared across all LR frames and **jointly optimized with the per-frame alignment parameters**. It requires no HR training data at all, reaches 8x upsampling, and works on both satellite and handheld imagery. Because it constrains the solution with genuinely sub-pixel-shifted views, it explicitly avoids the hallucinated structure that single-image SR produces. For an astronomy product this is architecturally near-ideal: continuous sky model, arbitrary output grid, no ground truth needed, per-frame geometry as free parameters.

**Multi-image SR in remote sensing** — the closest existing production analogue, because satellite revisits differ in time, illumination, and atmosphere just like your contributors. **HighRes-net** (Deudon et al., [arXiv:2002.06460](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.06460), recursive pairwise fusion + learned sub-pixel registration "ShiftNet"); **RAMS** (Salvetti et al., 3D residual attention, code [EscVM/RAMS](https://github.com/EscVM/RAMS)); **TR-MISR** (transformer fusion, code [Suanmd/TR-MISR](https://github.com/Suanmd/TR-MISR)); all benchmarked on the ESA **PROBA-V** super-resolution challenge dataset, which is the canonical multi-date, variable-N, variable-quality MISR benchmark.

**Unaligned reference guidance** — [Hyperspectral SR with real unaligned RGB guidance](https://arxiv.org/html/2302.06298) and its 2025 successor with two-stage optical-flow + warp alignment ([arXiv:2505.02109](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02109)) are the methodological template for "a high-quality reference exists but is not registered and not from the same sensor" — i.e. using a professional survey cutout as a reference for amateur frames.

**Self-supervised real-world SR** — [Self-Supervised Learning for Real-World SR from Dual and Multiple Zoomed Observations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.02171) and [LWay (CVPR 2024)](https://github.com/haoyuc/LWay) address the case where no ground truth exists and the degradation is unknown. Both are relevant because your repository will never have paired ground truth for most fields.

## 3. Astronomy-specific: the classical, rigorous foundation

This is where the mathematically correct answers already exist. **Do not reinvent these.**

**Drizzle (Variable-Pixel Linear Reconstruction)** — Fruchter & Hook, *PASP 114, 144 (2002)*, [DOI 10.1086/338393](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1086/338393), [arXiv:astro-ph/9808087](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2002PASP..114..144F/abstract). Developed for HST WFPC2, whose PSF was undersampled by its pixel grid — exactly the situation of a wide-field DSLR shot. Each input pixel is shrunk by a factor `pixfrac`, geometrically mapped onto a finer output grid through the distortion solution, and its flux distributed by area overlap. It **preserves photometry**, handles geometric distortion, weights by per-pixel statistical significance, and — given dithered inputs with sub-pixel diversity — recovers the sampling lost to aliasing. It is the single most important classical algorithm for this product. Implementation: [spacetelescope/drizzlepac](https://github.com/spacetelescope/drizzlepac) and the standalone [`drizzle`](https://pypi.org/project/drizzle/) package; also implemented in Siril, PixInsight and DeepSkyStacker.

**Optimal coaddition** — Zackay & Ofek, *ApJ 836, 187* ([arXiv:1512.06872](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017ApJ...836..187Z)) and *ApJ 836, 188* ([arXiv:1512.06879](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-4357/836/2/188)), "How to COAAD Images I & II". **This is the correct answer to "how do I combine images of unequal quality?"** and it is *not* a weighted average. Paper I proves that for point-source detection you must first apply a matched filter using **each image's own PSF**, then sum with weights proportional to F_j/sigma_j^2 (transparency over variance). Paper II derives a coadd that is a sufficient statistic — optimal for *any* subsequent measurement in the background-dominated limit — and shows it has a well-defined, narrower effective PSF. Given a corpus with seeing varying by 5x and sky brightness varying by 100x, using this instead of a sigma-clipped mean is worth a large fraction of a magnitude in depth, for free. Compare the practical trade-offs in Rubin/LSST's [DMTN-015 "Flavors of Coadds"](https://dmtn-015.lsst.io/), which contrasts PSF-matched, direct, and Kaiser (Pan-STARRS) coaddition.

**PSF matching / optimal image subtraction** — Alard & Lupton, *ApJ 503, 325 (1998)*, [DOI 10.1086/305984](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1086/305984); Alard, *A&AS 144, 363 (2000)* (spatially varying kernel, flux conserving). Solves for the convolution kernel that maps a good-seeing image onto a bad-seeing one, as a linear combination of Gaussian-modulated polynomial basis functions. **ZOGY** — Zackay, Ofek & Gal-Yam, *ApJ 830, 27 (2016)*, [arXiv:1601.02655](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.02655) — derives the statistically proper difference image from first principles, with a closed-form, symmetric, artifact-free result. Python implementations: [KerryPaterson/ZOGY](https://github.com/KerryPaterson/ZOGY), [GOTO-OBS/ZiP](https://github.com/GOTO-OBS/ZiP). This is how you build the transient/variability layer of the product.

**Resampling and mosaicking** — **SWarp** (Bertin et al. 2002; [user guide](https://www.star.herts.ac.uk/~pwl/Lucas/rho_oph/swarp.pdf)) and **Montage** ([arXiv:1005.4454](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.4454)), the two reference implementations of astrometrically-correct, flux-conserving reprojection onto a common grid; Montage is exact-overlap (photometrically rigorous), SWarp is interpolation-based (fast).

**Lucky imaging** — Law, Mackay & Baldwin, *A&A 446, 739 (2006)*, ["Lucky imaging: high angular resolution imaging in the visible from the ground"](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/pdf/2006/05/aa3695-05.pdf). Short exposures freeze atmospheric turbulence; a small percentage of frames are selected on a sharpness criterion and shift-and-added, routinely improving angular resolution by 2.5-4x over long-exposure seeing. Also [High-efficiency lucky imaging](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/432/1/702/1139352) (MNRAS 432, 702). **Conceptually crucial for your product**: a repository with thousands of contributions has a *long tail of excellent-seeing frames*, and heterogeneity therefore becomes an asset rather than a liability, provided the pipeline can select or weight on measured PSF quality.

**The Thresher: Lucky Imaging without the Waste** — Hitchcock, Bramich, Foreman-Mackey, Hogg, Hundertmark. *MNRAS 511, 5372 (2022)*, [arXiv:2202.04686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.04686). The state of the art conceptually, and the intellectual bridge to Tier C below. Instead of throwing away 90% of frames, it performs **online multi-frame blind deconvolution**: a single latent scene is optimized against a justified likelihood, with each frame's individual PSF modeled explicitly. Result: it gets lucky imaging's angular resolution *and* the SNR of using all the data. Applied to your problem, this is precisely the right objective function — one sky model, N heterogeneous forward models, one likelihood.

**Amateur-domain deconvolution in practice** — [BlurXTerminator](https://www.rc-astro.com/software/bxt/) (Russell Croman) is a ~45M-parameter neural network for semi-blind, spatially-varying PSF deconvolution, purpose-built for amateur data ([the mathematics](https://www.rc-astro.com/the-mathematics-of-blurxterminator/)). It is proprietary and PixInsight-only, but it is the de-facto benchmark your product will be compared against, and its existence proves the market accepts learned deconvolution in this domain.

## 4. Deep learning for astronomical restoration and super-resolution

**Deep learning for a space-variant deconvolution in galaxy surveys** — Sureau, Lechat, Starck. *A&A 641, A67 (2020)*, [arXiv:1911.00443](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.00443). Two strategies: **Tikhonet** (closed-form Tikhonov deconvolution followed by a U-Net denoiser) and an ADMM-unrolled iterative scheme. Tikhonet was both more accurate and far cheaper. Both beat classical sparse-recovery baselines on GREAT3 simulations with realistic PSFs.

**ShapeNet: Shape constraint for galaxy image deconvolution** — Nammour, Akhaury, Girard, Lanusse, Sureau, Ben Ali, Starck. *A&A 663, A69 (2022)*, [arXiv:2203.07412](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.07412), [Zenodo release](https://zenodo.org/records/5552714). Adds an explicit *shape* (moment) constraint to the loss so that the network preserves the ellipticity measurements that weak-lensing science depends on. **The principle generalizes: for scientific images, the loss must constrain the physically meaningful quantity (flux, shape, position), not just L1/PSNR.** Follow-up: [Deep learning-based galaxy image deconvolution](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/astronomy-and-space-sciences/articles/10.3389/fspas.2022.1001043/full) (Akhaury et al., Frontiers 2022).

**Diffusion Models for Probabilistic Deconvolution of Galaxy Images** — [arXiv:2307.11122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11122). A classifier-free conditional diffusion model captures a genuinely *diverse posterior* of possible deconvolutions, unlike a conditional VAE which collapses.

**Bayesian Deconvolution of Astronomical Images with Diffusion Models: Quantifying Prior-Driven Features in Reconstructions** — [arXiv:2411.19158](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.19158), NeurIPS 2024 ML4PS workshop, code [astrodeepnet/diffusion4astro](https://github.com/astrodeepnet/diffusion4astro). **Read this one for its methodology of honesty**: it uses Diffusion Posterior Sampling with a score-based prior trained on cosmological simulations, applied to real HSC images, and explicitly measures how much of the output structure came from the prior rather than the data. Any generative component in your product needs this kind of audit.

**Echoes in the Noise: Posterior Samples of Faint Galaxy Surface Brightness Profiles with Score-Based Likelihoods and Priors** — *AJ (2025)*, [IOPscience](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/1538-3881/adb039). Posterior sampling for PSF deconvolution on real HST data.

**Datasets and benchmarks:**
- **AstroSR** — Miao et al., *ApJS 274:7 (2024)*, data + eval code at [jiaweimmiao/AstroSR](https://github.com/jiaweimmiao/AstroSR). Real galaxy image pairs from **overlapping SDSS and HSC-SSP footprints** — i.e. genuine low-quality/high-quality pairs of the same objects from different telescopes. This is the exact recipe you should copy at the amateur->professional level.
- **STAR: A Benchmark for Astronomical Star Fields Super-Resolution** — Wu, Zhuang, Huang, Zhang, Ouyang, Lu. *NeurIPS 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks*, [arXiv:2507.16385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16385), code [GuoCheng12/STAR](https://github.com/GuoCheng12/STAR). 54,738 **flux-consistent** star-field pairs from HST with physically faithful LR counterparts, plus a Flux-Invariant Super-Resolution (FISR) model and — importantly — a *flux consistency metric*. The field has correctly concluded that PSNR is not a sufficient criterion for astronomical SR.
- **FluxFlow: Conservative Flow-Matching for Astronomical Image Super-Resolution** — Liu et al. (2026), [arXiv:2605.03749](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.03749v2). Flow matching with observation-uncertainty and source-importance weighting, plus a **test-time correction step that removes spurious sources**. Introduces the **DESI-HST dataset**: 19,500 real co-registered ground-to-space pairs with real atmospheric PSF variation. This is the closest published analogue to "turn ground-based images into space-quality images," and it is explicitly built around real, not simulated, atmospheric degradation.

**The most important point:** your repository generates its own best training data. A deep classical coadd of 500 contributions of M31 is a legitimate pseudo-ground-truth for the 5-frame subsets drawn from it.

**Radio-astronomy SR** (algorithmically relevant even though the physics differs): [First AI for deep super-resolution wide-field imaging in radio astronomy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11336) (Terris et al.), the **R2D2** residual-DNN-unrolled paradigm, [IRIS: Bayesian radio-interferometric imaging with score-based priors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.02473), [Learned radio interferometric imaging for varying visibility coverage](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.08958), and [Deep learning-based deconvolution for interferometric radio transient reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13909) (code: [bjmch/DL-RadioTransient](https://github.com/bjmch/DL-RadioTransient)). The recurring lesson: **unrolled physics-informed networks beat black-box networks, and generalize across varying measurement configurations** — which is exactly your variable-N, variable-geometry regime.

## 5. The Event Horizon Telescope analogy: what transfers and what does not

This deserves a blunt answer, because it is the most likely source of a fatally over-promised product claim.

**The core references.** **CHIRP** — Bouman, Johnson, Zoran, Fish, Doeleman, Freeman, "Computational Imaging for VLBI Image Reconstruction," *CVPR 2016*, [arXiv:1512.01413](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.01413). **eht-imaging** — Chael et al. (*ApJ 829, 11*, 2016; *ApJ 857, 23*, 2018), code [achael/eht-imaging](https://github.com/achael/eht-imaging), the standard library for regularized maximum likelihood (RML) VLBI imaging. **EHT Collaboration 2019, Paper IV**, *ApJL 875, L4*, [arXiv:1906.11241](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.11241), which describes the blind four-team CLEAN-vs-RML validation protocol. Recent extension with learned priors: [Event-horizon-scale Imaging of M87* under Different Assumptions via Deep Generative Image Priors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.02785).

### What does NOT transfer: aperture synthesis

VLBI achieves angular resolution lambda/B, where B is the baseline between telescopes rather than the diameter of any one of them, because each station records the **complex electric field** — amplitude *and* phase — against an atomic-clock time reference, and the signals are later correlated. Interfering two stations requires the relative timing to be stable to a small fraction of the wave period.

At 1.3 mm (EHT), one wave period is ~4 picoseconds; hydrogen masers make this achievable. At 550 nm optical, one period is **1.8 femtoseconds**. A consumer camera:

- records **intensity**, |E|^2, destroying phase irrecoverably at the moment of detection;
- integrates over 10^-3 to 10^2 seconds, i.e. 10^12 to 10^17 wave periods;
- has no absolute time reference better than ~seconds (NTP-synced phone clocks);
- sits under an independently fluctuating column of turbulent atmosphere.

Therefore: **you cannot synthesize an aperture from a repository of photographs. Not with more photos, not with better software, not ever.** Two people 1000 km apart photographing Andromeda do not form a 1000 km telescope. Their images combine *incoherently* — you are adding intensities, which improves statistics, not adding fields, which would improve resolution. Any product copy suggesting an "Event Horizon Telescope for amateurs" in the resolution sense is false.

**The one genuine exception, for completeness and honesty:** *intensity interferometry* (Hanbury Brown-Twiss) correlates photon-arrival *intensity* fluctuations rather than fields, and needs only nanosecond timing — 10^6 times easier. It has recently been demonstrated with **0.25 m amateur-class telescopes on Sirius** at a 3.3 m baseline in a suburban backyard: [Intensity interferometer results on Sirius with 0.25 m telescopes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.09894), *MNRAS 537, 2527 (2025)*. But it requires single-photon avalanche detectors, GPS-disciplined time-taggers, simultaneous observation, and works only on very bright, nearly unresolved sources. It is a hardware program, not an image-repository feature. It cannot be applied retroactively to stored photographs.

**Also not achievable:** resolving beyond the diffraction limit lambda/D of the *largest single contributing aperture*. Deconvolution can partially undo the blur *inside* the optical passband and can, with strong priors, extrapolate modestly outside it — but the optical transfer function is exactly zero beyond the cutoff, so anything reconstructed there is prior, not measurement.

### What DOES transfer: the RML/inverse-problem template

The genuinely valuable idea to steal from EHT is not the interferometry — it is the **reconstruction philosophy**:

> Do not "combine images." Posit a single latent sky model. Write an explicit forward model for how *each individual observation* was produced from that sky. Maximize (data-fidelity summed over all observations) + (regularizers). Sample the posterior. Report uncertainty. Validate blind, with multiple independent methods and synthetic-truth injection.

eht-imaging's `Imager` class is literally a weighted sum of per-observation data terms plus regularizing functionals (maximum entropy, total variation, TSV, L1 sparsity), minimized by gradient descent. Swap "complex visibility with station gains" for "CCD image with WCS + PSF + throughput + sky background + nonlinear response," and you have the correct architecture for your product. The EHT's blind-team validation protocol is also directly worth copying as a scientific-credibility mechanism.

### What you actually gain from N heterogeneous photographs

| Gain | Achievable? | Scaling / magnitude |
|---|---|---|
| Depth / SNR | **Yes, reliably** | proportional to sqrt(sum of t_i * throughput_i) in the background-limited regime, with Zackay-Ofek weighting. Hundreds of contributions ~ several magnitudes deeper than any single frame. |
| Recovering aliased detail (sub-pixel sampling) | **Yes** | This is the real SR win. Wide-field consumer setups are *badly undersampled* (a 50 mm lens on 4 um pixels gives ~16 arcsec/pixel against a ~2 arcsec optical limit), so there is a large gap between pixel sampling and optical cutoff to reclaim. Drizzle/MFSR can realistically deliver 1.5-3x effective linear sampling improvement given genuine sub-pixel diversity — which arrives free from independent observers. |
| Deconvolution sharpening | **Partially** | ~1.5-2x FWHM reduction with high SNR and a well-measured PSF. Prior-dependent beyond that; must be flagged. |
| "Lucky" seeing selection | **Yes** | A large corpus has a superb tail. Per-frame-PSF weighted fusion (Thresher-style) beats both naive averaging and hard frame-selection. |
| Dynamic range / HDR | **Yes** | Different exposures -> unsaturated cores plus deep outskirts (e.g. the Orion Trapezium). Easy, visually spectacular, under-exploited. |
| Multi-scale fusion | **Yes** | Short focal lengths constrain large-scale flux and faint extended emission; long focal lengths supply high spatial frequencies. Incoherent analogue of radio "feathering." |
| Time domain (variables, transients, asteroids, proper motion) | **Yes, and it is scientifically the most valuable output** | ZOGY differencing against your own proper coadd reference. |
| Parallax / depth | **Only for solar-system objects** | Earth's diameter (~12,742 km) gives ~1 degree parallax on the Moon, arcminutes on near-Earth asteroids, and meteor triangulation. For anything beyond the solar system it is identically zero. |
| Angular resolution beyond lambda/D | **No** | Physically excluded. |

## 6. Practical pipeline components (all open source, all production-ready)

**Astrometric calibration (plate solving).** [astrometry.net](https://astrometry.net) — Lang, Hogg, Mierle, Blanton, Roweis, *AJ 139, 1782 (2010)*, [arXiv:0910.2233](https://arxiv.org/abs/0910.2233). Blind solving via geometric hashes of four-star asterisms; returns a full WCS including SIP distortion, with no prior pointing knowledge. Runs locally with downloaded index files — essential, since you must not depend on a public web service for bulk ingest. [ASTAP](https://www.hnsky.org/astap.htm) is a faster alternative solver (and stacker) with compact star-database indices, widely used by amateurs and by NINA. EXIF focal length + sensor size gives a pixel-scale prior that speeds solving by orders of magnitude.

**Registration.** [astroalign](https://github.com/quatrope/astroalign) — Beroiz, Cabral, Sanchez, *Astronomy & Computing 32, 100384 (2020)*, [arXiv:1909.02946](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.02946). Triangle-similarity matching on detected sources; finds an affine transform without WCS. Use it as a fallback when plate solving fails, and as a cross-check.

**Reprojection.** [`reproject`](https://github.com/astropy/reproject) — three algorithms: interpolation (SWarp-like), **exact spherical-polygon overlap** (Montage-like, strictly flux-conserving), and `reproject_adaptive` implementing DeForest (2004) anti-aliased adaptive resampling, which is the right default when changing scale substantially. [`drizzle`](https://pypi.org/project/drizzle/) and [drizzlepac](https://github.com/spacetelescope/drizzlepac) for true drizzle. [dfreproject](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.03932) is a recent GPU-accelerated alternative.

**Photometry, PSF, background.** [`photutils`](https://photutils.readthedocs.io) (source detection, `Background2D` for spatially varying sky, ePSF construction, PSF photometry), [`sep`](https://github.com/kbarbary/sep) (SExtractor's core as a fast library), [`ccdproc`](https://github.com/astropy/ccdproc) (bias/dark/flat, `wcs_project`, and `Combiner` with sigma-clipping and per-pixel masks), and `astropy` itself for WCS, FITS, units, and coordinate transforms.

**Photometric standardization across heterogeneous cameras.** This is the piece most amateur pipelines get wrong and it is essential here. **Gaia DR3 synthetic photometry** (Montegriffo et al., *A&A 674, A33*, 2023) provides flux-calibrated spectra for ~200M sources, from which you can synthesize magnitudes in *any* passband within 330-1050 nm — including an arbitrary consumer camera's R/G/B response. See also [RGB photometric calibration of 15 million Gaia stars](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.08734) and [Comparing photometric calibration of DESI imaging and Gaia synthetic photometry](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.16630). This gives you a per-image, per-channel zero point, color term, and atmospheric extinction coefficient — the machinery that makes a modified-Ha DSLR and an unmodified mirrorless combinable on a common flux scale.

**Reference stackers to benchmark against and interoperate with.** [Siril](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.03346) (free, cross-platform, scriptable, includes drizzle and a plate solver — the best open-source baseline), DeepSkyStacker, PixInsight WBPP, ASTAP, Astro Pixel Processor.

**Prior art for multi-observer stacking as a service.** [Citizen Science Astronomy with a Network of Small Telescopes](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.04337) (Unistellar): a real pipeline that dark-subtracts, plate-solves and stacks images uploaded by many observers across continents. Worth studying for operational lessons.

## 8. Optical and atmospheric constraints -> required metadata schema

Physics that will corrupt a naive stack, and the metadata needed to model it:

- **Seeing** varies from ~0.7 arcsec (exceptional) to >5 arcsec. Sets each frame's PSF FWHM. -> Must be *measured* per image from stars, not trusted from any reported value.
- **Airmass** X ~ sec(z). Drives (a) atmospheric extinction, ~0.15-0.3 mag/airmass in V at a decent site, more in blue; (b) **differential chromatic refraction** — the atmosphere disperses light, so a broadband PSF is *elongated along the parallactic angle*, increasingly at low altitude, and the elongation differs per color channel. This directly corrupts both photometry and PSF-matched coaddition ([VIMOS atmospheric dispersion analysis](https://www.aanda.org/articles/aa/full_html/2014/06/aa23459-14/aa23459-14.html)). Mitigation: model per channel, or down-weight/reject high-airmass frames.
- **Field rotation.** Alt-azimuth or untracked mounts rotate the field during an exposure, producing a *spatially varying* rotational blur that grows linearly with distance from the field center. A single global PSF model is wrong for such frames.
- **Light pollution** (Bortle 1-9 / SQM mag per arcsec^2) sets an additive, spatially varying, spectrally biased background. It must be modeled and subtracted *before* weighting, or the SNR weights are meaningless. Moon phase, altitude and angular separation from target matter as much as the ground-based Bortle class.
- **Transparency, cloud, dew, haze** — vary within a single session.
- **Sensor and encoding.** JPEG is 8-bit, gamma-encoded, chroma-subsampled and lossy; it is *not linear in flux*, so stacking JPEGs without inverting the tone curve destroys photometric validity and biases the coadd. RAW is strongly preferred; JPEG-only contributions need an estimated inverse-response curve and should be flagged as photometrically unreliable.
- **Spectral response.** IR-cut filters differ; Ha-modified DSLRs have radically different red response; narrowband filters (Ha/OIII/SII) are a different measurement entirely and must be kept in separate channels.

**Minimum per-photo schema for the repository:**

*Declared / EXIF:* UTC timestamp (sub-second where possible); observer latitude, longitude, altitude; camera make/model, sensor size, pixel pitch, bit depth; lens or telescope focal length and aperture (f/#); filter(s); ISO/gain, offset, exposure time; number of sub-exposures and whether already stacked, and with what software; RAW vs JPEG and the original file if possible; white balance and color matrix as shot; calibration frames applied (bias/dark/flat); mount type (fixed / tracker / EQ / alt-az, guided or not); dither pattern if any.

*Derived by the pipeline (and this is the part that actually matters):* full WCS with distortion (CRVAL, CD matrix, SIP); pixel scale and position angle; measured PSF FWHM, ellipticity and orientation, mapped across the field; sky background level, gradient model and estimated SQM; limiting magnitude; photometric zero point, color term and extinction coefficient against Gaia; per-pixel variance map; airmass and parallactic angle at the exposure midpoint; moon phase/altitude/separation; star-trailing metric; saturation mask; a scalar quality score used for weighting.

## 9. What this means for the product

### Tier A — Classical stacking that works today (0-6 months, low risk, high certainty)

Build a fully classical, physically correct pipeline. It works, it is defensible, and it delivers most of the achievable gain.

1. **Ingest and linearize.** Decode RAW (`rawpy`/LibRaw) to linear counts; for JPEG-only contributions, estimate and invert the tone curve and mark the frame as photometrically degraded.
2. **Plate solve** every image with a local astrometry.net index set or ASTAP, seeded by the EXIF focal length. Reject unsolvable frames. This is your entire registration solution and it is better than any learned alignment.
3. **Characterize** each frame: detect sources (`sep`), fit a spatially varying PSF (`photutils` ePSF), fit the sky background (`Background2D`), compute a variance map, measure the zero point / color term / extinction against Gaia DR3 synthetic photometry.
4. **Cluster** images into targets by WCS footprint overlap and choose a common output tangent-plane grid. Pick the output pixel scale from the distribution of contributing scales and the measured sub-pixel dither diversity — not arbitrarily.
5. **Coadd properly.** Reproject with `reproject_adaptive` or drizzle (`pixfrac` ~ 0.6-0.8). Weight per pixel following **Zackay & Ofek**: matched-filter each frame with its own PSF, weight by (throughput / variance), then sum. Add robust per-pixel outlier rejection for planes, satellites, and cosmic rays. Optionally produce a second, PSF-homogenized coadd for photometry.
6. **Deliver** linear 32-bit FITS with a full weight map, uncertainty map, effective PSF, and complete provenance (which images, from whom, with what weight), plus a stretched preview for humans.
7. **Free extras that will surprise users:** HDR compositing from the exposure spread; per-target depth curves showing SNR vs number of contributors; a difference-image (ZOGY) layer that flags asteroids, variable stars and transients across the corpus.

*Expected honest claim:* "Several magnitudes deeper than any single contribution, with recovered detail that no individual frame contains, and calibrated photometry." That is true, verifiable, and already a strong product.

### Tier B — Learned multi-frame SR fine-tuned on your own data (6-18 months)

1. **Build pairs automatically.** (i) Internal: Tier-A deep coadds serve as pseudo-HR; random small subsets serve as LR. (ii) External: cross-match your fields against HST/JWST/HSC/DESI Legacy cutouts for true HR ground truth — the AstroSR and FluxFlow recipe.
2. **Choose an architecture that tolerates your regime.** Start from **RBSR** (variable N, recurrent) or **Burstormer/BSRT** (strong fusion) but **delete the alignment stage and substitute WCS-driven warping**; you already know the geometry to sub-pixel accuracy, which is a decisive advantage over consumer burst SR. For truly heterogeneous scales and orientations, **Magnet++**'s graph formulation is the right inductive bias. For a training-data-free path with arbitrary output scale, **SuperF**'s implicit-field test-time optimization is the strongest candidate and worth a serious prototype.
3. **Condition on physics.** Feed the network each frame's PSF kernel, noise sigma map, zero point, background, and airmass as explicit side inputs. Physics-informed / unrolled formulations (deep-rep, R2D2) consistently generalize better across varying measurement configurations than black-box fusion.
4. **Use scientific losses.** Flux consistency (STAR/FISR), shape/moment constraints (ShapeNet), and a data-fidelity term evaluated *through the forward model* against the original frames — not only L1 against a pseudo-HR.
5. **Validate adversarially.** Inject synthetic sources of known magnitude and check recovery linearity; verify no output source lacks a counterpart in Gaia/PanSTARRS; publish per-pixel uncertainty; run held-out fields where true HST/JWST truth exists. Ship a "confidence" overlay so users can see which structure is measured and which is inferred.
6. **Product framing:** the learned stage should be an *optional, clearly-labelled enhancement layer* over the Tier-A scientific coadd — never a silent replacement for it.

### Tier C — Research-grade (18 months+, high ambition, genuinely novel)

The defensible long-term idea, and the correct one:

**A single, continuously-updated Bayesian sky model, reconstructed jointly from the entire repository.**

- Represent the sky as a continuous field (implicit neural representation a la SuperF, or a multi-resolution pixel/spline basis on a tangent plane, possibly HEALPix-tiled globally).
- Give every contributed photograph its own explicit forward operator: WCS + distortion, spatially varying PSF (including field rotation and differential chromatic refraction), spectral response, throughput, background, sensor nonlinearity and saturation.
- Optimize a **Thresher-style multi-frame blind deconvolution objective at repository scale**: sum over images of a proper likelihood, plus regularizers — structured exactly like eht-imaging's RML `Imager` (total variation, TSV, entropy, wavelet/starlet L1), but in image space rather than visibility space.
- Refine the per-image nuisance parameters (PSF, gains, astrometry) *jointly* with the sky — self-calibration, the one EHT technique that genuinely does transfer.
- Add a **score-based/diffusion prior with posterior sampling** for uncertainty quantification, following diffusion4astro's protocol of explicitly measuring how much structure the prior contributed. Publish that number.
- Extend to **multi-scale fusion** — wide-field short-focal contributions constrain large-scale flux and faint extended emission; long-focal contributions constrain fine structure. This is the incoherent analogue of radio feathering, and it is a real research contribution in its own right.
- Extend to **4D**: a time-varying sky model over a decade of contributions enables variable-star light curves, proper motions, asteroid recovery, and transient discovery — using a Zackay-Ofek proper coadd as the reference and ZOGY as the differencing operator. **This, not prettier pictures, is where a photo repository could produce publishable science and become genuinely unique.**

### What to never promise

- Any Event Horizon Telescope-style resolution gain from combining separated observers. Incoherent intensity addition does not synthesize an aperture. Say so plainly in the product's own documentation; it will build credibility with the amateur astronomy community, which is technically sophisticated and allergic to overselling.
- Resolution beyond the diffraction limit of the best contributing optic (except by modest, prior-dependent deconvolution, which must be labelled).
- Parallax or 3D depth for anything outside the solar system.
- Unflagged generative output. In astronomy a hallucinated point source is not an aesthetic defect — it is a false discovery.
